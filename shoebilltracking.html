The legendary Shoebill — one of Africa's most sought-after birds

By Terrain Team | Wetland Birding

Encounter Africa's Most Prehistoric Bird

Standing up to 1.5 metres tall with its distinctive clog-shaped bill and intense, unblinking gaze, the Shoebill stork (Balaeniceps rex) is one of the most extraordinary birds on Earth. Often described as looking like a relic from the dinosaur age, this enigmatic wetland dweller is the ultimate prize for birders worldwide.

Uganda offers some of the world's best opportunities to observe Shoebills in their natural habitat. Glide silently through papyrus-lined channels in traditional canoes, guided by expert local trackers who know every hidden corner of the wetlands. The thrill of finally locking eyes with a Shoebill is an experience that stays with you forever.

The Species

About the Shoebill

Height

1.1 to 1.5 metres tall, making it one of Africa's tallest birds.

Wingspan

Impressive 2.3 to 2.6 metre wingspan for silent flight over wetlands.

Diet

Primarily lungfish, catfish, and occasionally small crocodiles or water snakes.

Status

Classified as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List with an estimated 5,000-8,000 individuals.

Top Shoebill Tracking Locations

Mabamba Bay Wetland

Located on the shores of Lake Victoria near Entebbe, Mabamba is Uganda's premier Shoebill destination. This Ramsar-designated wetland hosts a resident population of Shoebills and offers the highest success rate for sightings. Traditional canoe excursions depart early morning for the best encounters.

45 minutes from Entebbe | 90% success rate

Lake Mburo Wetlands

The extensive wetlands surrounding Lake Mburo National Park provide excellent Shoebill habitat. Combining a Shoebill tracking excursion with a full safari experience in the park creates an unforgettable dual adventure of birding and wildlife viewing.

Western Uganda | Combine with safari

Semuliki Valley Wetlands

The remote wetlands of the Semuliki Valley in western Uganda offer a more adventurous Shoebill tracking experience. This off-the-beaten-path location combines rare bird sightings with the stunning backdrop of the Rwenzori Mountains.

Remote location | Fewer crowds

Lutembe Bay & Ziwa Area

Alternative wetland sites near Kampala offer Shoebill sightings with the added convenience of proximity to the capital. These locations are ideal for travelers with limited time who still want to experience this iconic species.

Near Kampala | Half-day trips

Frequently Asked Questions

What are my chances of seeing a Shoebill?

At Mabamba Bay, our success rate is approximately 90% during the dry season (December to February, June to August). Shoebills are resident birds in this wetland, meaning they don't migrate. However, sightings are never guaranteed as these are wild animals in their natural habitat. Early morning departures significantly increase your chances.

How long does a Shoebill tracking excursion take?

A typical Shoebill tracking excursion lasts 3-4 hours, including transport from Entebbe or Kampala to the wetland, the canoe trip itself, and return. The actual time on the water is usually 1.5-2 hours. We recommend combining this with other activities for a full day experience.

Is Shoebill tracking suitable for children?

Yes, Shoebill tracking is suitable for children aged 8 and above who are comfortable in canoes and can sit quietly for extended periods. The experience is educational and exciting for young nature enthusiasts. Life jackets are provided for all participants, and our guides ensure safety throughout the excursion.

What other birds might I see during the excursion?

Mabamba Bay is a birding paradise with over 300 recorded species. Besides the Shoebill, you're likely to see Papyrus Gonolek, Blue-headed Coucal, African Jacana, Malachite Kingfisher, and many other wetland specialists. Our guides will help you identify species along the way, making it an excellent introduction to Ugandan birdlife.

How does my visit support conservation?

Your visit directly funds wetland conservation and community development. Fees pay local community guides, support habitat protection initiatives, and provide sustainable livelihoods that reduce pressure on the wetland ecosystem. Mabamba Bay's designation as a Ramsar site and community-based tourism model has been crucial in protecting the resident Shoebill population.
